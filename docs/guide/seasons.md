# Seasons and training modes

## The training year

The year is divided into blocks — three, four or six, your choice, with four as
the default. You pick what runs in each. A season's calendar window is derived
from the blocks it lists, so the dates and the eligibility cannot drift apart.

The default rotation lands the athletic block over summer and the mass block
over winter:

**Spring** FEROX Recomp → **Summer** Greek Fire → **Autumn** Bridge →
**Winter** Winter Fire

## The twelve seasons

| Season | For | Calories |
|---|---|---|
| **Greek Fire** | Athletic power, low body fat | Maintenance, sometimes under |
| **Winter Fire** | Muscle mass and thickness | Controlled surplus |
| **FEROX Recomp** | Muscle and fat loss at once | Maintenance, protein high |
| **Clean Bulk** | Maximum muscle gain | Surplus, 300–500 over |
| **The Cut** | Fat loss, muscle retention | Deficit, 400–600 under |
| **Bridge** | Recovering between hard blocks | Maintenance |
| **Foundation** | A first block, or a first block back | Maintenance |
| **Iron Base** | Maximal strength | Slight surplus |
| **Hybrid** | Strength and endurance together | Maintenance |
| **Tempo** | Aerobic capacity, a race in the diary | Maintenance, carbs up |
| **Peak Week** | Looking and performing your best on one day | Maintenance |
| **Reset** | Movement quality, fixing what hurts | Maintenance |

FEROX Recomp is eligible for all four blocks, which is what makes it the
year-round default.

## Training modes — the part that actually changes the sessions

A season says how hard each set is (rep range, rest). What it *also* has to say
is how **many**, and those two move in opposite directions. A block built on
triples needs five sets of them to accumulate anything; a block built on
fifteens needs three or it becomes junk volume.

That axis runs the whole way across:

| Mode | Reps | Sets on main lifts | The bar | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | 1–5 | 5 | 100% | Iron Base, Peak Week |
| **Power** | 4–8 | 5 | 92% | Greek Fire |
| **Hypertrophy** | 6–12 | 4 (+ an extra accessory) | 95% | Winter Fire, Clean Bulk |
| **Balanced** | 6–10 | 4 | 95% | Recomp, Foundation, Bridge, Hybrid |
| **Metabolic** | 10–15 | 3 | 85% | The Cut |
| **Endurance** | 8–12 | 3 | 85% | Tempo |
| **Quality** | 10–15 | 3 | 70% | Reset |

`The bar` scales the prescribed weight on top of whatever the rep count already
implies — a high-rep block is not just more reps, it is a genuinely lighter bar.

## The split changes too

Each mode reshapes the week rather than just renumbering it:

- **Strength** strips trailing accessory work. A strength day is four hard lifts,
  not eight movements.
- **Hypertrophy** adds an isolation slot to every day. That is where the extra
  volume goes.
- **Power** leads every day with jumps, while the nervous system is fresh, and
  closes the last day with sprints.
- **Metabolic** finishes every lifting day on conditioning — in a deficit the
  calorie work belongs *after* the weights, not instead of them.
- **Endurance** rebuilds the week as two maintenance lifting days plus runs.
- **Quality** swaps power work for single-limb and mobility work.

The exercises change with the mode as well. Greek Fire reaches for box jumps,
medicine ball slams and kettlebell swings; Iron Base reaches for a barbell and
will not hand you a depth jump at all.

## Switching

Seasons → pick a block → pick a season. Your plan rebuilds immediately, and so
do your calorie targets. Nothing you have logged is affected.
