# What weight to lift

This is the part most apps leave blank, and it is the single most common reason
people give up on one. A training log that opens every weight field at zero is
asking you to already know the answer.

## Before you have lifted it

FEROX estimates a one-rep max from four things — your bodyweight, your sex, your
age and your stated experience — against a table of what each movement typically
costs relative to a barbell. It then works backwards to the weight you could
manage for the prescribed reps with a couple left in the tank.

**And then it takes 15% off.**

That undershoot is deliberate and it is not timidity. It is an asymmetry:

- A first session that is 10 kg light costs you thirty seconds. You add a plate
  and carry on.
- A first session that is 10 kg heavy costs you a failed rep, possibly a tweaked
  shoulder, and — most often — it quietly convinces you this app is not for you.

Everything in the model leans the same way. Estimates sit at the middle of the
published bands rather than the top. Rounding is always *down* to a weight you
can actually load. Progression has to be earned.

## Once you have lifted it

The moment you log a real set, the estimate stops mattering for that lift. FEROX
uses your best set instead, and then:

| What happened last time | What happens next |
|---|---|
| You hit **every** prescribed rep | The weight goes up — one increment, or two on lower-body barbell lifts, which have far more room |
| You were **one or two reps short** | Same weight. Finish the reps first |
| You **stalled**, more than two short | Back off about 10% and build again |

That is linear progression. For everybody below "very advanced" it is still the
thing that works, and it is the thing you can explain in one sentence: hit your
reps and the weight goes up.

## Lifts you have never done

Here is the part that makes the difference between a table and a plan.

If you have never done a lat pulldown but you *have* been logging rows and
pulldowns, FEROX knows something about your back. Each muscle group is measured
against what the standards predicted for you, and that ratio carries across to
movements you have never performed.

So a strong back opens a new pulling exercise heavier than the textbook, and a
weak one opens it lighter. It is clamped — a deadlift at double the estimate is
real information about your back and it is *not* permission to open an untried
lift at double the textbook weight — and nothing happens at all until you have
logged at least two lifts in that group. One lift is an anecdote.

## Weight, and volume, go opposite ways

Two things run in opposite directions on purpose:

- A muscle group that is **ahead** earns **heavier weight**.
- A muscle group that is **behind** earns **an extra set**.

A lagging group needs more work, not a heavier version of the work it is already
failing at. You can see all of this on **Workouts → Your plan**, in the Strength
profile card.

## Where to see the numbers

Every exercise in the catalog shows its estimated one-rep max for you, next to
your actual best if you have one, with the percentage between them. Workouts →
**Exercises**, search for anything.
